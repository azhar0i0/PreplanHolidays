Rome to Florence to Venice by Train: Times, Fares and Stations (2026)

PHBy The Preplan Holidays planning team · Published September 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Italy's high-speed trains are the reason a multi-city trip works. Two operators, Trenitalia's Frecciarossa and the private Italo, run city-centre to city-centre with no security lines, no luggage fees and, if you book ahead, fares cheaper than a taxi to the airport. Here are the times, prices and stations for the four cities we book.

Fares are per person in standard class on Frecciarossa or Italo, checked September 2026. The cheapest "Super Economy" and Italo "Smart" tickets appear when booking opens, usually 120 days ahead for Trenitalia and around 90 for Italo, and disappear first. Base (fully flexible) fares are always available and are what you will pay if you book on the day.

Which station

  • Rome: Roma Termini for almost everything. Some trains also call at Roma Tiburtina; check your ticket. Termini is a 20-minute walk or 5-minute taxi from our Trevi hotel.
  • Florence: Firenze Santa Maria Novella (SMN), in the centre. Our Florence hotel is seven minutes on foot. A few Italo trains stop only at Firenze Rifredi in the suburbs; avoid those.
  • Venice: Venezia Santa Lucia, on the island, at the top of the Grand Canal. Trains also stop at Venezia Mestre on the mainland ten minutes earlier; stay on. From Santa Lucia, vaporetto line 1 or 2 takes you to San Marco and our Grand Canal hotel.
  • Milan: Milano Centrale is the main station. Some Frecciarossa and Italo services use Milano Porta Garibaldi, which is beside our Milan hotel, or Milano Rogoredo. All three are on the metro.

How to book

Buy on trenitalia.com or italotreno.com (both have English sites and take US cards) or through the Trenitalia and Italo apps. Tickets are e-tickets; show the QR code on your phone. Seats are reserved on every high-speed train, so the ticket shows your carriage and seat number. If you would rather not deal with it, we add train tickets to any multi-city package for $45 per person per leg and put them in your voucher pack.

Frecciarossa or Italo?They are equally fast and comfortable. Italo is often €5 to €10 cheaper on the same route; Frecciarossa has more departures and is slightly more reliable at the busiest times. Book whichever is cheaper at the time you want.

On the day

  • Arrive 15 to 20 minutes before departure. Platforms are announced about 10 minutes ahead on the boards; there is no check-in.
  • Luggage goes in racks at the ends of each carriage or overhead. There are no weight limits and no fees, but you carry it yourself; one suitcase each is sensible.
  • Standard class is fine. Premium and Business add a wider seat and a snack for €10 to €20 more; worth it on the three-hour Rome to Venice run, not on the short hops.
  • Strikes happen a few times a year and are announced in advance; high-speed trains usually run a guaranteed minimum service. We watch the calendar for every trip we book.

Airport trains

The same principle applies at either end. From Fiumicino, the Leonardo Express reaches Roma Termini in 32 minutes for €14. From Malpensa, the Malpensa Express reaches Porta Garibaldi and Centrale in about 50 minutes for around €13. Florence airport is 20 minutes from SMN by tram for €2, and Venice's airport connects by Alilaguna boat to San Marco in about 75 minutes for €18. Our airport transfers guide covers each in detail.
