Milan gets sold short. It has Leonardo's Last Supper, the third-largest cathedral in Europe with a walkable roof, the country's best aperitivo culture and, in Brera and Navigli, two of Italy's most pleasant neighbourhoods to wander. It is also the airport hub most US flights use and the northern end of the high-speed line, which makes it the natural first night of a longer trip.

Where you stay: Holiday Inn Milan Garibaldi Station

The Holiday Inn Milan Garibaldi Station is on Via Ugo Bassi, beside Porta Garibaldi station and the Porta Nuova skyscrapers. We book the Double Standard Room with breakfast for two adults and request twin beds if you prefer them; connecting rooms are available for families. The hotel is LGBTQ+ friendly. Garibaldi is on metro lines M2 and M5, so the Duomo is ten minutes away, and the Malpensa Express stops here, so you can reach the airport without changing.

Three house rules. Breakfast covers up to two adults per room; a third guest pays for breakfast at the hotel. Every guest, children included, shows a government photo ID or passport at check-in, which is Italian law rather than hotel policy. And cash payments at the hotel are capped at €5,000 by Italian regulation, which will not trouble most people but is worth knowing.

Milan city tax in 2026

Milan raised its imposta di soggiorno for the Winter Olympics year. From 1 April to 31 December 2026 the rates are the highest in Italy, charged per person per night for up to 14 nights and paid at the hotel. The council has said the increase is limited to 2026; the 2027 rates had not been published when we last checked.

Getting in

From Milan Malpensa (MXP), Trenord's Malpensa Express runs every 15 to 30 minutes to Milano Centrale via Porta Garibaldi in about 50 minutes; the adult fare is around €13 (check the current price on the Malpensa Express site, which was reviewing fares in 2026). From Linate (LIN), metro line M4 reaches the centre in 12 minutes, then M2 to Garibaldi. Bergamo (BGY), used by low-cost airlines, is an hour by coach to Centrale. By train, Milano Centrale is the main high-speed station; some Italo and Frecciarossa services use Porta Garibaldi directly.

What to book before you fly

  • The Last Supper. Reservation is always mandatory and tickets are released in four-month blocks on the Cenacolo Vinciano site; the September to December 2026 block went on sale in June. A small number of extra tickets are released every Wednesday at noon for the following week. If the official site is sold out, a guided tour that includes entry is the reliable fallback.
  • Duomo rooftop. Buy the lift ticket online for a timed slot; the stairs option is cheaper and rarely sells out.
  • La Scala. Museum entry is walk-up; performances need booking months ahead.

How to spend two nights

Arrive, drop bags and take the metro to the Duomo for the roof at golden hour, then walk through the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II to La Scala and up into Brera for dinner. Day two: the Last Supper at your reserved time, Sforza Castle and Parco Sempione, then an aperitivo crawl along the Navigli canals. With a spare day, Lake Como is under an hour away by train from Centrale to Varenna, and Bergamo's upper town is 50 minutes.

When to go

April to June and September to October are best. Milan Fashion Week (late February and late September) and the Salone del Mobile design fair in April push hotel prices up sharply, so avoid those weeks unless you are going for them. August is quiet; many Milanese leave the city and a surprising number of restaurants close for two weeks around Ferragosto.

Milan as part of a longer trip

Milan is where most of our northern routes begin. Milan and Venice is a four-night pairing linked by a 2-hour 15-minute train; the Northern Italy circuit adds Florence; and The grand Italian four runs nine nights from Milan down to Rome so you fly into one airport and out of another.
