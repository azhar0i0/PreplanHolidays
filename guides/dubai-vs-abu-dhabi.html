Dubai or Abu Dhabi? Which to Visit and How Many Days in Each

PHBy The Preplan Holidays planning team · Published September 19, 2026 · 6 min read

They are 90 minutes apart by road and could hardly be more different in feel. Dubai is the spectacle: the tallest building on earth, seven-star hotels, malls with ski slopes. Abu Dhabi is the capital: quieter, greener, with the country's two great cultural set-pieces. Most people should see both, and the only real question is the split.

What Dubai does best

  • The skyline. The Burj Khalifa's 124th-floor deck at sunset, the Dubai Fountain show below it, and the Marina at night.
  • Beaches and resorts. Jumeirah and the Palm have the best hotel beaches in the Gulf.
  • Old Dubai. The Creek, the abra boat crossing for one dirham, the gold and spice souks, and the restored Al Fahidi quarter.
  • The desert. An afternoon dune drive, camel ride and barbecue dinner at a camp, included in our package.
  • Shopping and food. Dubai Mall is a destination in itself; the food scene runs from Emirati to Michelin.

What Abu Dhabi does best

  • Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque. One of the most beautiful modern buildings anywhere; go at dusk. Free, with dress rules (abayas are lent at the door).
  • Louvre Abu Dhabi. Jean Nouvel's domed museum on Saadiyat Island, with a world collection under a rain of light.
  • Yas Island. Ferrari World, Warner Bros. World and the Formula 1 circuit, which is why families often reverse the split.
  • Space. Wide corniche, calmer traffic, a more Emirati feel.

How many days

Our seven-night package gives Dubai five nights and Abu Dhabi two, which suits most first-time visitors: three full days for Dubai's sights, beach and desert, one full day for the mosque and the Louvre, plus the travel day. Families with children who want Yas Island should ask us for four and three. Abu Dhabi can also be done as a long day trip from Dubai, but you will see the mosque at midday instead of dusk, and the Louvre will be rushed.

When to go

November to March is the season: 24 to 30°C, low humidity, sea warm enough to swim. December and January are the priciest. April and October are hot but workable. May to September is 40°C and humid; hotels drop rates by half and life moves indoors, which suits some people fine. Ramadan (expected February to early March in 2027) is a fascinating time to visit but restaurants close by day and alcohol service is limited; we plan around it.

Practical notes for US travellers

  • US passport holders get a free visa on arrival in the UAE; passports need six months' validity.
  • Dress modestly away from the beach and cover shoulders and knees at the mosque. Alcohol is served in licensed hotels and restaurants.
  • Tipping 10% is customary in restaurants; taxis are metered and cheap; the Dubai Metro is spotless and reaches the Burj Khalifa, the Marina and the airport.
  • Fridays are the weekend; the souks and some attractions open later.
