7-Day Italy Itinerary: Rome, Florence and Venice by Train

PHBy The Preplan Holidays planning team · Published September 19, 2026 · 8 min read

This is the itinerary behind our most-booked package, Italy's big three: three nights in Rome, two in Florence, two in Venice, travelling north by high-speed train. It assumes you land in Rome and fly home from Venice. Reverse it if your flights run the other way.

Day 1: Arrive in Rome

Land at Fiumicino, take the Leonardo Express to Termini (32 minutes, €14) and a short taxi to the hotel near the Trevi Fountain. Drop the bags. Walk to the Trevi Fountain, the Pantheon and Piazza Navona as the light goes, and eat near Campo de' Fiori. Do not plan anything that needs a ticket today; jet lag wins.

Day 2: Ancient Rome

Colosseum at your reserved slot (book on the official site 30 days ahead, €18), then the Forum and Palatine Hill on the same ticket. Lunch in Monti. Afternoon at the Capitoline Museums or a slow walk over the Circus Maximus to the Aventine keyhole. Evening: Trastevere for dinner.

Day 3: The Vatican

Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel at opening time (book 60 days ahead, €20 plus €5 fee), then St Peter's Basilica; take the dome climb if your legs allow. Afternoon in the Prati district for lunch, then the Castel Sant'Angelo or the Borghese Gallery if you reserved it. Last night in Rome: an early evening at the Spanish Steps, then dinner near the hotel.

Day 4: Train to Florence

Breakfast, check out, pay the city tax (€7.50 per adult per night at a four-star). Frecciarossa or Italo from Termini to Firenze SMN, 1 hour 19 minutes on the fastest service; book two months ahead for a €20 fare. Walk seven minutes to the hotel. Afternoon: Piazza del Duomo, the Baptistery doors, the campanile climb. Evening: cross the Ponte Vecchio and eat in the Oltrarno.

Day 5: Renaissance Florence

Uffizi at your reserved slot (book a month ahead, €25 plus €4 fee). Lunch at the Mercato Centrale. Afternoon: the Accademia for Michelangelo's David if reserved, or Santa Croce and the leather workshops. Sunset from Piazzale Michelangelo; it is a 25-minute walk up or a short bus ride. Last dinner in Florence.

Day 6: Train to Venice

Breakfast, check out, pay the city tax (€7 per adult per night). Train from SMN to Venezia Santa Lucia, about 2 hours; stay on past Mestre. From the station, vaporetto line 1 the full length of the Grand Canal to San Marco Vallaresso (40 minutes; buy a 24-hour pass for €25). Check in at the hotel on the canal. Evening: St Mark's Square after the day visitors have gone.

Day 7: Venice

St Mark's Basilica at opening with a timed ticket (€10, or €30 for the full visit), then the Doge's Palace. Late morning on the Rialto market side of the bridge. Afternoon: either Murano and Burano by vaporetto line 12, or a slow wander through Cannaregio and the Jewish Ghetto. Sunset spritz on the Zattere. If you are travelling on an access-fee day, remember your hotel-guest exemption QR code from cda.ve.it.

Day 8: Home

Breakfast on the Grand Canal terrace, pay the city tax (€4.50 per adult per night), and take the Alilaguna boat from the San Marco stop to Marco Polo airport (75 minutes, €18) or a water taxi (30 minutes, about €130 per boat). Allow three hours before a US flight and extra time for the Entry/Exit System checks on departure.

What it costs

The hotel package with breakfast is from $1,363 per person (two sharing) as Italy's big three. Add about €95 per couple for city taxes, €40 to €60 per person for two advance train tickets, and roughly €80 per person for the four museum reservations above. Flights from the east coast run $700 to $1,200 return in spring and autumn. Our Italy trip cost guide has a full budget.

Swaps that work

  • More Rome: take a night from Venice and give it to Rome for the Borghese and the Appian Way.
  • Add Milan: if you can find two more nights, fly into Milan and out of Rome; that is the 10-day itinerary.
  • Tuscany day: a Siena day trip from Florence on day 5 instead of the Accademia.
