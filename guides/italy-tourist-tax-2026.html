Italy Tourist Tax 2026: Rome, Florence, Venice and Milan Rates Explained

PHBy The Preplan Holidays planning team · Published September 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Every Italian city charges an overnight tourist tax, the imposta di soggiorno. It is never included in a package price, ours or anyone else's, because by law the hotel collects it from you directly. This guide gives the 2026 rates for the four cities we book, who is exempt, and how to budget for it.

How the tax works

The tax is charged per person, per night, and the rate depends on the type and star rating of your accommodation. Each city caps the number of nights it applies to. You pay at check-out, in cash or by card, and the hotel gives you a receipt. Packages, online travel agents and hotel prepayments do not include it, and a hotel that tells you otherwise is mistaken.

Quick budgetFor a couple staying in four-star hotels, expect about €15 per night in Rome, €14 in Florence, €9 in Venice and €20 in Milan (2026 rates). A seven-night Rome, Florence and Venice trip comes to roughly €95 for two.

Venice

Venice's overnight tax varies by star rating, location and season. The historic-centre, high-season hotel rates are below; they are reduced by 30% from 1 to 31 January, by 30% on the mainland (Mestre and Marghera) and by 20% on the lagoon islands outside the centre. The tax applies for a maximum of 5 consecutive nights.

The Venice access fee is different

On top of the overnight tax, Venice charges day visitors an access fee (contributo di accesso) on selected peak days. In 2026 it ran on 60 days between 3 April and 26 July, from 8:30am to 4pm, at €5 if booked at least four days ahead or €10 otherwise, for anyone aged 14 and over entering the historic centre. Hotel guests are exempt but must register for a QR exemption on cda.ve.it. Our Venice access fee guide has the full rules. The 2027 calendar had not been announced when this was last updated.

Milan (2026 Olympic-year rates)

Milan raised its tax for the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics in two steps. From 1 January 2026 four- and five-star hotels moved to €10; from 1 April to 31 December 2026 (resolution 144 of 12 February 2026) the rates below apply. They are the highest in Italy, apply for up to 14 consecutive nights, and the council has stated the increase is limited to 2026. Rates for 2027 had not been published at the time of writing.

Practical tips

  • Carry some euros in cash for the tax; most hotels take cards but a few smaller ones prefer cash.
  • Keep the receipt. The tax is a municipal charge and a legitimate hotel will always issue one.
  • If a hotel adds the tax to a prepaid package invoice, query it. It should be a separate line paid locally.
  • Children's exemptions are by age on the night of the stay; bring ID for the children, which the hotel needs anyway for police registration.
