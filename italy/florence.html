Florence is compact enough to see properly in two days and dense enough that you will want to come back. The whole Renaissance centre, from the Duomo to the Uffizi to the Ponte Vecchio, is a fifteen-minute walk end to end, which is why we put you a few streets from the main station rather than out in the hills: you arrive by train, walk to the hotel, drop the bags and are in front of the cathedral before you have finished your first gelato.

Where you stay: Grand Hotel Adriatico

The Grand Hotel Adriatico is on Via Maso Finiguerra, between Santa Maria Novella station and the Arno. We book the Comfort Room with breakfast included and request a double bed for you; the hotel does its best, though bedding is not guaranteed. The front desk is open 24 hours, so a late train from Rome or a delayed flight into Peretola is never a problem. Local city tax is paid directly to the hotel and is not part of the package rate.

The one rule to remember: same-day cancellations and no-shows are charged in full. If your plans change, tell us as early as you can and we will ask the hotel what is possible.

Florence city tax in 2026

Florence updated its imposta di soggiorno on 1 February 2025 and the same rates apply in 2026. It is charged per person per night for a maximum of 7 consecutive nights and paid at the hotel.

Getting in

Most of our guests arrive by train. Firenze Santa Maria Novella (SMN) is the high-speed station, 1 hour 19 minutes from Rome and about 2 hours from Venice on the fastest Frecciarossa or Italo services, and the hotel is a seven-minute walk from the platforms. If you fly into Florence Peretola (FLR), tram line T2 runs from the terminal to the Unità and Alamanni stops beside SMN in about 20 minutes; a standard 90-minute ticket costs €2. From Pisa airport, the Pisamover shuttle plus a regional train to SMN takes about an hour and a quarter.

What to book before you fly

  • Uffizi Gallery. €25 in high season on the official site, plus a €4 reservation fee if you book a time slot, which you should. Rick Steves' advice, which matches ours: reserve the Uffizi about a month ahead from April to October.
  • Accademia (Michelangelo's David). Book roughly two months ahead in peak season; the €4 booking fee is worth every cent against the standby line.
  • Brunelleschi's dome. Climbing the cathedral dome requires a timed reservation with the Brunelleschi Pass; the cathedral floor is free and unreserved.

How to spend two nights

Arrive, check in and walk to Piazza del Duomo for the evening. The next morning climb the dome or the campanile early, cross Piazza della Signoria to the Uffizi for your reserved slot, then walk over the Ponte Vecchio into the Oltrarno for lunch. Finish at Piazzale Michelangelo for sunset over the city. If you have a second full day, use it for the Accademia and the San Lorenzo market, or take the 15-minute train to Prato or the hour to Siena.

When to go

Late April to June and September to October are ideal; July and August are hot and crowded around the Duomo. Florence's patron-saint day, 24 June, brings fireworks over the Arno and the historic football final in Piazza Santa Croce, and hotels fill for it. Winter is quiet and museum queues almost disappear; January sales on Via de' Tornabuoni are a bonus.

Florence as part of a longer trip

Because Florence sits in the middle of the Rome to Milan line, it is the city we most often add to another. Rome and Florence is the classic five-night pairing; Florence and Venice suits people who want art and water; Italy's big three adds both. Every combination uses the same hotel and includes breakfast.
