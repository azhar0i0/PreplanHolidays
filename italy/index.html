Italy's four great cities sit on one high-speed railway line. Milan to Venice is about two and a quarter hours, Venice to Florence two, Florence to Rome an hour and twenty minutes. That geography is why we built our Italy range the way we did: one good hotel in each city, and packages that combine them in every sensible order. Pick a single city for a long weekend, or string two, three or all four together and let the trains do the work.

How our Italy packages work

Every Italy package is a hotel stay with breakfast, priced per person based on two sharing, in US dollars. The hotels are the same four in every combination: the Trevi Collection Hotel in Rome, the Grand Hotel Adriatico in Florence, Hotel Monaco & Grand Canal in Venice and the Holiday Inn Milan Garibaldi Station. Multi-city prices are 7% lower than booking the same nights separately. Flights are not included, so you can use points, fly open-jaw into Milan and out of Rome, or add flights through us. Train tickets between cities can be added for $45 per leg per person, or you can book them yourself on Trenitalia or Italo.

You pay once, in advance, and receive a prepaid voucher for each hotel plus a day-by-day plan. At the hotel the only thing left to pay is the local city tax, which by law is collected at the desk and which we list in euros for every stay before you book.

Which cities, and how many nights

If it is your first time, Rome, Florence and Venice in that order is the route most people are happiest with: three nights, two nights, two nights. Add Milan at the start if you are flying in there, which most US flights do, or if you want the Last Supper and Lake Como. Skip Milan if you have only a week. Our guide to how many days each city needs goes through the trade-offs, and the 7-day and 10-day itineraries show the two combinations we book most.

When to go to Italy

April, May, September and October give the best balance of weather and crowds in all four cities. July and August are hot and, around 15 August, half-closed. November to March is cheap and quiet, with Venice's Carnival and Milan's fashion weeks as the expensive exceptions. Book spring and autumn dates six to twelve weeks ahead; the Venice hotel in particular fills early. See best time to visit Italy for the month-by-month version.

Entering Italy from the US in 2026

US citizens need a passport valid for at least three months beyond their planned departure from the Schengen area (we recommend six) and no visa for stays up to 90 days. The EU's Entry/Exit System has been fully operational since April 2026, so your first entry involves a fingerprint and photo registration at the border; allow extra time at Fiumicino or Malpensa. The ETIAS travel authorisation was not yet in operation in September 2026 and is expected to start in 2027; when it does it will cost €20 and last three years. We tell every client the current status when we confirm a trip.
