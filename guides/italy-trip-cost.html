How Much Does a Trip to Italy Cost in 2026? A Real Budget for Two

PHBy The Preplan Holidays planning team · Published September 19, 2026 · 7 min read

People ask us this more than anything except how many days to spend in each city, and the honest answer is that it depends on three things: when you go, how you fly, and how much you eat sitting down. Here is a real budget for two adults on a seven-night Rome, Florence and Venice trip in spring or autumn 2026, using our hotel package and paying everything else ourselves.

Where the money goes, and how to spend less

Flights are the biggest variable. Spring and autumn from New York, Boston or Washington run $700 to $1,200 return per person; from the west coast add $200 to $400. Flying into Milan is often $100 cheaper than Rome, which is one reason our 10-day itinerary starts there. Tuesday and Wednesday departures are consistently cheapest.

Hotels are the second. Our package uses four-star hotels in the centre of each city; you could halve the hotel line with two-star rooms near the stations, and you would spend the difference on taxis and time. Travelling in November, January or February cuts hotel rates by a third with no change in what you see.

Food is where Italy is generous. A sit-down lunch of pasta and a glass of wine is €15 to €20 per person away from the main squares; the same lunch on Piazza Navona or St Mark's Square is €40 and worse. Breakfast is included in every night of our packages, which quietly saves €15 to €25 per person per day.

Tickets are cheap for what they are. Book on the official sites (linked from each of our city pages) and you pay the face price plus a small reservation fee; third-party "skip the line" resellers charge two to three times as much for the same entry.

The costs people forget

  • City tax, paid at each hotel, €4.50 to €10 per adult per night. See our tourist tax guide.
  • Card fees. Use a card with no foreign transaction fee and always choose to pay in euros, not dollars, when the terminal offers.
  • Tips. Not expected. Round up a taxi fare or leave a few euros for exceptional service; the coperto cover charge on a restaurant bill is not a tip.
  • Luggage. Most US carriers include one checked bag on transatlantic flights, but budget carriers within Europe do not. Left-luggage at stations is €6 to €10 per bag per day.
