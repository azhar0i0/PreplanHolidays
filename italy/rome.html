Rome is the city most first-time visitors to Italy start with, and for good reason: two thousand years of buildings are stacked on top of each other inside a centre you can cross on foot. The trick is to sleep in the middle of it. Our Rome hotel sits between the Trevi Fountain and the Spanish Steps, which puts the Pantheon, Piazza Navona and the Via del Corso within ten minutes of the front door and the Colosseum within twenty-five.

Where you stay: Trevi Collection Hotel

The Trevi Collection Hotel is on Via Capo le Case, a quiet street of the historic centre a few hundred metres from the Trevi Fountain and Piazza di Spagna. We book the Standard Quadruple Room, which sleeps up to four and works equally well for a couple who want the space, two friends sharing, or a family. Breakfast is included every morning. A double bed is requested for you and a cot is available for babies under two (16 euros per night, paid at the hotel).

Two things to know before you arrive. The hotel may ask for a cash or card deposit at check-in as a guarantee; it is returned when you leave, minus any extras. And five or more rooms on the same dates count as a group booking, which the hotel confirms separately at its own rates, so tell us early if you are travelling with a crowd.

Rome city tax in 2026

Rome's contributo di soggiorno has been at the same level since 1 October 2023. It is charged per person, per night, for the first 10 nights of a stay, and you pay it directly to the hotel at check-out. It is never included in a package price, ours or anyone else's.

Getting in from the airport

From Rome Fiumicino (FCO) the Leonardo Express train runs non-stop to Roma Termini every 15 minutes and takes 32 minutes; tickets are €14 each and one child aged 4 to 12 travels free with each paying adult. From Termini the hotel is a 5-minute taxi or a 20-minute walk. Rome's fixed taxi fare from Fiumicino to anywhere inside the Aurelian walls is €55 (at the time of writing) for up to four people with luggage, which is often the better deal for three or four travellers. From Ciampino (CPA), used by some European low-cost airlines, the fixed taxi fare is €40. We can add a private airport pickup to any package for $38 per person.

What to book before you fly

  • Colosseum, Forum and Palatine. Only buy from the official Parco archeologico del Colosseo site. The standard 24-hour ticket is €18 and slots are released 30 days ahead, at the time of the slot, Rome time. Arena-floor and underground tickets go on sale seven days ahead and sell out within minutes.
  • Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel. Tickets are €20 plus a €5 booking fee on the Vatican's own site, released 60 days ahead. The museums close on Sundays except the last Sunday of the month, when entry is free and the queue is enormous.
  • St Peter's Basilica is free to enter but the security line can take an hour by mid-morning. Go at 7am or in the last hour before closing.
  • Borghese Gallery admits a fixed number of people every two hours and must be reserved.

How to spend three nights

Our three-night package gives you two full days plus an arrival evening and a departure morning, which is exactly the right amount of time for Rome's headline sights without rushing. Day one is ancient Rome: the Colosseum first thing, then the Forum and Palatine Hill, lunch in Monti, and an evening walk from the Pantheon to Piazza Navona and back to the Trevi Fountain after dark, when the crowds thin. Day two is the Vatican in the morning, then Trastevere across the river for the afternoon and dinner. If you have a third full day, spend it on the Borghese Gallery and the Appian Way, or take the train to Ostia Antica.

When to go

April, May, September and October have the best weather and the biggest crowds. July and August are hot, often above 35°C, and many neighbourhood restaurants close around Ferragosto on 15 August, although the Colosseum, Vatican Museums and other state sites stay open. November to February is the cheapest and quietest time, with mild days and the occasional rain; Rome never really closes. Easter week and the days around 29 June, the feast of Saints Peter and Paul, bring big crowds to St Peter's.

Rome as part of a longer trip

Rome sits at the southern end of the high-speed line, so it makes the natural first or last stop of a multi-city trip. Florence is 1 hour 19 minutes away on the fastest Frecciarossa, Venice about 3 hours 40 minutes and Milan about 3 hours. If you land in Rome and fly home from Milan or Venice, an open-jaw ticket saves a day of backtracking. See our guide to how many nights each city deserves and the train guide for connections.
